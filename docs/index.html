Capture

The screenshot tool Windows should have shipped.

Press ⊞ Shift S, pick a region, a window or the whole screen, mark it up and press Done. It’s already in your library.

  • Region, window, full screenWindows highlight as you hover and a click captures. Lock the region to 16:9, 9:16, 4:3, 3:2, 1:1 or 21:9 while you drag.
  • A real annotation editorPen, shapes, arrows, text, emoji, crop and paste-as-layer, in an overlay right after the shot.
  • A library that files itselfDone saves automatically to the folder you choose. Or skip the editor and auto-save and copy every shot.
  • Freeze or liveFreeze the screen while you select, or keep it live so motion stays visible.
  • Share to a phone or TVShow your screen in a browser on the same Wi-Fi, straight from the capture overlay.

Record

Never miss the play.

Screen recordings from the same overlay, and game recording that sits quietly in the background until something worth keeping happens. Uses your graphics card’s hardware encoder (NVIDIA, AMD or Intel), so your frame rate stays yours.

Instant replay

Keeps the last few minutes in the background. Press Alt F10 and the moment that just happened becomes a clip.

Background recording

Records whole sessions, optionally only while a game is in focus. Open a session on a timeline, scrub it, and cut clips later.

Hotkey recording

Ctrl F11 starts and stops. Drop timeline markers while you play with Ctrl F12. Hotkey recordings are never deleted automatically.

Clip editor

Trim and crop. Home/End jump to the ends, I/O set in and out at the playhead. Export Discord-friendly files.

Audio, separated

Game, Discord voice chat and microphone as separate sources with their own volume, mic noise suppression, and optional separate tracks for editing.

Share as a link

One click uploads a clip to catbox.moe and copies a link: no account, links don’t expire, 200 MB cap. Only when you ask.

Call

Voice and screen sharing, PC to PC.

Make a room, share its code, talk. Share your screen at the resolution, frame rate and bitrate you choose. There’s no account to make and no Toru server in the middle: the audio and video go straight between your PCs.

Rooms use a reusable 16-character code. Signaling is sealed with a key derived from it.

  • Screen share with no capsAny resolution, 1–500 fps, 0.5–500 Mbps. Hardware encoding on NVENC, AMF or Quick Sync, in H.264 or AV1, tuned for low latency.
  • AI noise suppressionDeepFilterNet3 runs locally on your PC and takes out keyboard clicks while your voice stays at full level. Off, Standard or AI, switchable mid-call.
  • Auto bitrateFollows your resolution and frame rate, so 1080p60 and an ultrawide at 144 fps each get a sensible rate. Type a number to go manual.
  • Per-person volume, 0–200%Turn up the quiet friend. Saved per person.
  • No echo of the call in your streamYour stream’s desktop audio leaves out Toru, so call voices aren’t sent back to viewers, even with audio mixers like SteelSeries Sonar.
Good to know: there’s no relay server yet, so two people who are both behind very strict NATs may not be able to connect. Voice is a full mesh, which suits a handful of people. Screen sharing starts from inside a call.

LAN · New in v0.13

Your room is now a LAN party.

Turn on LAN and everyone in your room joins one virtual network. LAN and direct-play games find each other as if you were all in the same house. No Radmin VPN, no Hamachi, no extra app, and the room code you already shared is the network name and password.

  1. Join a roomCreate or join one on the Call page. The LAN card sits right there, even before you’re in a room.
  2. Set up onceThe first time, Windows asks for administrator approval one time to add the Toru LAN adapter. After that, LAN turns on and off with no prompts.
  3. Turn LAN on, then start the gameEach friend gets a fixed address like 100.96.12.34. Host a LAN game; everyone else sees it in the list.

Encrypted end to end

Traffic between friends is encrypted from one PC to the other. Each PC’s private key stays on that PC, readable only by the LAN service.

Direct, peer to peer

PCs connect straight to each other. Only LAN traffic uses the adapter; your normal internet traffic never goes through it.

Safe defaults

File sharing, RPC, NetBIOS, Remote Desktop and WinRM are blocked between members by default. Spoofed broadcasts are dropped and broadcast is rate-limited.

Built for games that look for each other on the local network

LAN copies game broadcasts and multicast to everyone in the room, so server lists fill in. By design, that covers games like these:

Minecraft Java (LAN worlds) Minecraft Bedrock Warcraft 3 StarCraft / Brood War Age of Empires 2 Factorio Terraria Stardew Valley Valheim Diablo 2 IPX classics via IPXWrapper

This list comes from how each game finds servers, not from testing every title on Toru. Games that need raw Ethernet frames (like IPXWrapper’s “Real IPX” mode) won’t work. Details in docs/lan.md.

Honest limits: LAN is direct-only for now, with no relay, so two PCs that are both behind very strict NATs may not connect. It needs that one-time administrator approval. While LAN is on, games that broadcast look for servers on Toru LAN rather than your home network.

And the rest of the toolbox

Small tools, done properly.

Send Files

Send any file, folder or capture with a short spoken code. The other person types it and the file goes straight to them, end-to-end encrypted. They can use Toru, the standard wormhole receive command, or scan the code as a QR on a phone.

Auto Clicker

Click a mouse button or press a key up to 500 times a second (1,000 if you ask), at the cursor or through points you pick. Ctrl Shift Y toggles or holds.

Stops itself at screen corners and edges, in stop zones you draw, on Alt+Tab, or when the wrong app is in front. Presets import from Blur AutoClicker.

Auto Clicker guide →

Notifications that help

Stacked, clickable cards that never steal focus and stay out of your captures, with buttons like Open, and short sounds with their own volume.

Library cleanup

Optional auto-delete: keep at most N captures, delete after N days, or both. Off by default. Multi-select delete for the rest.

Updates that ask first

New versions download in the background and announce themselves. Restart when you like, or they install when you quit. Toru keeps checking while it runs.

Privacy and open source

Your stuff stays yours.

Toru is MIT licensed and every line is on GitHub. There’s no account, no analytics and no telemetry in the code. Here is everything it talks to on the internet, and when.

Questions

Is Toru really free?

Yes. It’s MIT licensed open source. There’s no paid tier and nothing to sign up for.

Does it work on macOS or Linux?

No. Toru is Windows only. It leans on Windows capture APIs, hardware encoders and the Windows network stack.

Why does LAN ask for administrator approval?

Adding a network adapter and its firewall rules needs it. Toru asks once, when you first set LAN up, and installs a small service that only starts when you turn LAN on. After that, turning LAN on and off needs no prompts. Uninstalling Toru asks once more to remove it.

Can my friends and I always connect?

Usually. Calls and LAN connect directly between PCs. There’s no relay server yet, so if both of you are behind very strict NATs the connection may fail. Send Files can fall back to a relay, so it isn’t affected.

Where do my screenshots and recordings go?

To a library folder you pick in Settings. Game clips land in Videos\Toru Replays, sorted by game. Nothing is uploaded unless you click share.
